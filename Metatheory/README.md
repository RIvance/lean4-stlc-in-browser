# Typing and interpreter safety

We study a simply typed, call-by-value calculus with unit, natural numbers,
booleans, functions, and general recursion. Typing and evaluation use the same
expression syntax. Source locations are omitted from the notation; the results
hold for arbitrary locations attached to expressions.

## 1. Syntax and typing

Let $x$ range over variable names, $n$ over natural numbers, and $b$ over booleans.
Types, optional type annotations, and expressions are given by

$$
\begin{aligned}
\tau,\sigma &::= \mathsf{Unit}\mid\mathsf{Nat}\mid\mathsf{Bool}\mid\tau\to\sigma,\\
a &::= \bullet\mid\tau,\\
e &::= x\mid()\mid n\mid b\mid\lambda x:\tau.\,e\mid e_1\,e_2\\
  &\quad\mid\mathsf{let}\ x=e_1\ \mathsf{in}\ e_2
  \mid\mathsf{fix}^{a}\ x.\,e\mid e_1;e_2\\
  &\quad\mid\mathsf{if}\ e_1\ \mathsf{then}\ e_2\ \mathsf{else}\ e_3
  \mid e_1\mathbin{\odot}e_2.
\end{aligned}
$$

Here $\bullet$ denotes an absent annotation. The operators $\odot$ are addition,
truncated subtraction, multiplication, natural-number equality, and less-than
comparison. Write $\mathrm{res}(\odot)$ for $\mathsf{Nat}$ in the first
three cases and $\mathsf{Bool}$ in the last two.

A context $\Gamma$ is a finite ordered sequence of bindings. In $\Gamma,x:\tau$,
the new binding shadows any earlier binding for $x$. The judgment
$\Gamma\vdash e:\tau$ is the least relation generated by the following rules.

$$
\frac{\Gamma(x)=\tau}{\Gamma\vdash x:\tau}
\qquad
\frac{}{\Gamma\vdash():\mathsf{Unit}}
\qquad
\frac{}{\Gamma\vdash n:\mathsf{Nat}}
\qquad
\frac{}{\Gamma\vdash b:\mathsf{Bool}}
$$

$$
\frac{\Gamma,x:\tau\vdash e:\sigma}
     {\Gamma\vdash\lambda x:\tau.\,e:\tau\to\sigma}
\qquad
\frac{\Gamma\vdash e_1:\tau\to\sigma\quad\Gamma\vdash e_2:\tau}
     {\Gamma\vdash e_1\,e_2:\sigma}
$$

$$
\frac{\Gamma\vdash e_1:\tau\quad\Gamma,x:\tau\vdash e_2:\sigma}
     {\Gamma\vdash\mathsf{let}\ x=e_1\ \mathsf{in}\ e_2:\sigma}
\qquad
\frac{\Gamma,x:\tau\vdash e:\tau\quad a\in\{\bullet,\tau\}}
     {\Gamma\vdash\mathsf{fix}^{a}\ x.\,e:\tau}
$$

$$
\frac{\Gamma\vdash e_1:\tau\quad\Gamma\vdash e_2:\sigma}
     {\Gamma\vdash e_1;e_2:\sigma}
\qquad
\frac{\Gamma\vdash e_1:\mathsf{Nat}\quad\Gamma\vdash e_2:\mathsf{Nat}}
     {\Gamma\vdash e_1\mathbin{\odot}e_2:\mathrm{res}(\odot)}
$$

$$
\frac{\Gamma\vdash e_1:\mathsf{Bool}\quad\Gamma\vdash e_2:\tau\quad\Gamma\vdash e_3:\tau}
     {\Gamma\vdash\mathsf{if}\ e_1\ \mathsf{then}\ e_2\ \mathsf{else}\ e_3:\tau}
$$

All bindings are monomorphic. In particular, every use of the recursive variable
in a fixed point has the same type as the whole fixed point.

## 2. Inference and certification

A certificate is a type together with a derivation $D:\Gamma\vdash e:\tau$.
Let $H$ and $R$ be finite lists of candidate types for fixed points, and let
$H\cdot R$ denote list concatenation. The structural certificate checker
$\mathcal C$ traverses expressions in preorder, from left to right, consuming
one candidate type at each fixed point. It checks the premises of the typing
rules and returns a certificate together with the unused suffix, or rejects.

### Theorem. *Soundness and completeness of certification*

For every context $\Gamma$, expression $e$, and candidate list $H$,

$$
\mathcal C(\Gamma,e,H)=((\tau,D),R)
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
D:\Gamma\vdash e:\tau.
$$

Conversely, every derivation can be certified using a suitable finite list of
candidate types, independently of the unused suffix:

$$
D:\Gamma\vdash e:\tau
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\exists H.\ \forall R.\
\mathcal C(\Gamma,e,H\cdot R)=((\tau,D),R).
$$

*Proof.* Soundness follows because a successful check constructs a derivation
using the corresponding typing rule at every node. For completeness, induct on
$D$. At a fixed point, prepend its type to the candidates for its body. At every
other node, concatenate the candidate lists for the premises in traversal
order. Each induction hypothesis leaves the remaining suffix untouched.

Let $\mathcal I(\Gamma,e)\Downarrow\tau$ mean that inference successfully
returns $\tau$. Inference solves monomorphic constraints, rejects unresolved
recursive types, and certifies the resulting candidate types before accepting.

### Theorem. *Soundness of inference*

For every $\Gamma$, $e$, and $\tau$,

$$
\mathcal I(\Gamma,e)\Downarrow\tau
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\Gamma\vdash e:\tau.
$$

*Proof.* Successful inference supplies a certificate for the original expression
at the returned type. Apply the soundness direction of *Soundness and
completeness of certification*.

Completeness of certification does not imply completeness of inference.
For example, $\mathsf{fix}^{\bullet}\ x.\,x$ has a declarative derivation at
every type, while inference requires an annotation when no surrounding
constraints determine that type.

## 3. Command sequences

A command is an expression $e$ or a declaration $x:=e$. Declarations extend the
context for subsequent commands. Recursive surface definitions first expand
into fixed points; for example,

$$
\mathsf{def}\ f\,(x:\tau)=e
\quad\rightsquigarrow\quad
f:=\mathsf{fix}^{\bullet}\ f.\,(\lambda x:\tau.\,e).
$$

Multiple parameters expand into nested abstractions in their written order.
An annotated command sequence $A$ pairs each command with the type of its
expression. Its typing judgment is defined by

$$
\frac{}{\Gamma\vdash_{\mathrm P}[]}
\qquad
\frac{\Gamma\vdash e:\tau\quad\Gamma\vdash_{\mathrm P}A}
     {\Gamma\vdash_{\mathrm P}(e:\tau)::A}
$$

$$
\frac{\Gamma\vdash e:\tau\quad\Gamma,x:\tau\vdash_{\mathrm P}A}
     {\Gamma\vdash_{\mathrm P}(x:=e:\tau)::A}.
$$

Let $|A|$ erase these command annotations. For an unannotated sequence $P$,
its expression translation $[\![P]\!]$ is

$$
\begin{aligned}
[\![[]]\!] &= (),\\
[\![[e]]\!] &= e,\\
[\![e::P]\!] &= e;[\![P]\!]
  &&\text{if }P\ne[],\\
[\![(x:=e)::P]\!]
  &=\mathsf{let}\ x=e\ \mathsf{in}\ [\![P]\!].
\end{aligned}
$$

Write $T(A)$ for the annotation of the last command when that command is an
expression, and for $\mathsf{Unit}$ when the sequence is empty or ends in a
declaration. Let $\mathcal I_{\mathrm P}(\Gamma,P)\Downarrow A$ denote successful
inference for an entire command sequence, allowing later uses to constrain the
types of earlier declarations.

### Theorem. *Soundness and expression translation of command sequences*

For every $\Gamma$, $P$, and $A$,

$$
\mathcal I_{\mathrm P}(\Gamma,P)\Downarrow A
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\Gamma\vdash_{\mathrm P}A\ \land\ |A|=P.
$$

Moreover, every typed annotated sequence has a typed expression translation:

$$
\Gamma\vdash_{\mathrm P}A
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\Gamma\vdash[\![|A|]\!]:T(A).
$$

*Proof.* Certification checks each command under the context established by
its predecessors and preserves the command sequence. For the second statement,
induct on the sequence typing derivation. A declaration uses the let rule; a
nonfinal expression uses sequencing; a final expression uses its own derivation.
The empty continuation has type $\mathsf{Unit}$.

## 4. The closure machine

Values are unit, naturals, booleans, and closures
$\langle\lambda x:\tau.\,e,\rho\rangle$. An environment $\rho$ is a finite
ordered sequence of entries. Each entry is either a stored value
$\mathrm{val}(v)$ or a suspended recursive binding
$\mathrm{rec}(x,e,\rho)$. Extension, written $\rho[x\mapsto r]$, shadows
earlier bindings for $x$.

Value typing $\vdash_{\mathrm V}v:\tau$, entry typing
$\vdash_{\mathrm R}r:\tau$, and environment agreement $\rho\models\Gamma$ are
defined mutually. Literals have their respective base types, and

$$
\frac{\rho\models\Gamma\quad\Gamma,x:\tau\vdash e:\sigma}
     {\vdash_{\mathrm V}\langle\lambda x:\tau.\,e,\rho\rangle:\tau\to\sigma}
\qquad
\frac{\vdash_{\mathrm V}v:\tau}
     {\vdash_{\mathrm R}\mathrm{val}(v):\tau}
$$

$$
\frac{\rho\models\Gamma\quad\Gamma,x:\tau\vdash e:\tau}
     {\vdash_{\mathrm R}\mathrm{rec}(x,e,\rho):\tau}
\qquad
\frac{}{\varnothing\models\varnothing}
\qquad
\frac{\rho\models\Gamma\quad\vdash_{\mathrm R}r:\tau}
     {\rho[x\mapsto r]\models\Gamma,x:\tau}.
$$

These are inductive judgments over finite data. A recursive entry retains the
environment preceding its own binding, so its typing derivation is finite.

### Lemma. *Environment lookup*

For every $\rho$, $\Gamma$, $x$, and $\tau$,

$$
\rho\models\Gamma\ \land\ \Gamma(x)=\tau
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\exists r.\ \rho(x)=r\ \land\ \vdash_{\mathrm R}r:\tau.
$$

*Proof.* Induct on the context and its matching environment. If the newest
binding has name $x$, use the entry typing premise. Otherwise, use the induction
hypothesis for the remaining bindings.

### Frames and configurations

A frame $F$ records pending work; $\square$ marks the position awaiting a value.
The judgment $\vdash_{\mathrm F}F:\tau\Rightarrow\sigma$ describes a frame
that accepts type $\tau$ and produces type $\sigma$. The rules are specified
by the following table; captured environments must agree with the indicated
typing contexts.

| Frame $F$ | Premises | Input $\Rightarrow$ output |
| --- | --- | --- |
| $(\square\,e,\rho)$ | $\rho\models\Gamma$, $\Gamma\vdash e:\tau$ | $(\tau\to\sigma)\Rightarrow\sigma$ |
| $v\,\square$ | $\vdash_{\mathrm V}v:\tau\to\sigma$ | $\tau\Rightarrow\sigma$ |
| $(\mathsf{let}\ x=\square\ \mathsf{in}\ e,\rho)$ | $\rho\models\Gamma$, $\Gamma,x:\tau\vdash e:\sigma$ | $\tau\Rightarrow\sigma$ |
| $(\square;e,\rho)$ | $\rho\models\Gamma$, $\Gamma\vdash e:\sigma$ | $\tau\Rightarrow\sigma$ |
| $(\mathsf{if}\ \square\ \mathsf{then}\ e_1\ \mathsf{else}\ e_2,\rho)$ | $\rho\models\Gamma$, $\Gamma\vdash e_1:\tau$, $\Gamma\vdash e_2:\tau$ | $\mathsf{Bool}\Rightarrow\tau$ |
| $(\square\mathbin{\odot}e,\rho)$ | $\rho\models\Gamma$, $\Gamma\vdash e:\mathsf{Nat}$ | $\mathsf{Nat}\Rightarrow\mathrm{res}(\odot)$ |
| $v\mathbin{\odot}\square$ | $\vdash_{\mathrm V}v:\mathsf{Nat}$ | $\mathsf{Nat}\Rightarrow\mathrm{res}(\odot)$ |

A continuation $\kappa$ is a stack of frames, with the next frame first.
Continuation typing composes their input and output types:

$$
\frac{}{\vdash_{\mathrm K}[]:\tau\Rightarrow\tau}
\qquad
\frac{\vdash_{\mathrm F}F:\tau\Rightarrow\sigma\quad
      \vdash_{\mathrm K}\kappa:\sigma\Rightarrow\upsilon}
     {\vdash_{\mathrm K}F::\kappa:\tau\Rightarrow\upsilon}.
$$

A configuration $M$ is either $\langle e,\rho,\kappa\rangle$, evaluating an
expression, or $\langle v,\kappa\rangle$, returning a value. Its result type is
given by

$$
\frac{\rho\models\Gamma\quad\Gamma\vdash e:\tau\quad
      \vdash_{\mathrm K}\kappa:\tau\Rightarrow\sigma}
     {\vdash_{\mathrm M}\langle e,\rho,\kappa\rangle:\sigma}
\qquad
\frac{\vdash_{\mathrm V}v:\tau\quad\vdash_{\mathrm K}\kappa:\tau\Rightarrow\sigma}
     {\vdash_{\mathrm M}\langle v,\kappa\rangle:\sigma}.
$$

### Transitions

Write $M\longmapsto M'$ when one interpreter step succeeds with configuration
$M'$. Evaluation proceeds from left to right. A literal becomes its value,
and an abstraction becomes a closure capturing the current environment.
Application, let, sequencing, a conditional, and a binary operation first
evaluate their first subexpression, pushing the corresponding frame from the
table above. When a value reaches a frame, evaluation proceeds as follows:

| Pending frame | Action on the returned value $v$ |
| --- | --- |
| $(\square\,e,\rho)$ | Evaluate $e$ in $\rho$, with frame $v\,\square$. |
| $\langle\lambda x:\tau.\,e,\rho\rangle\,\square$ | Evaluate $e$ in $\rho[x\mapsto\mathrm{val}(v)]$. |
| $(\mathsf{let}\ x=\square\ \mathsf{in}\ e,\rho)$ | Evaluate $e$ in $\rho[x\mapsto\mathrm{val}(v)]$. |
| $(\square;e,\rho)$ | Evaluate $e$ in $\rho$. |
| $(\mathsf{if}\ \square\ \mathsf{then}\ e_1\ \mathsf{else}\ e_2,\rho)$ | If $v$ is true, evaluate $e_1$ in $\rho$; if false, evaluate $e_2$. |
| $(\square\mathbin{\odot}e,\rho)$ | Evaluate $e$ in $\rho$, with frame $v\mathbin{\odot}\square$. |
| $n\mathbin{\odot}\square$ | For a natural $v=m$, return $n\mathbin{\odot}m$. |

Each action removes the pending frame and retains the remaining continuation.
Arithmetic uses the usual operations on natural numbers, with subtraction
$n\mathbin{\dot{-}}m=\max(n-m,0)$; comparisons return booleans.
Calling a nonclosure, branching on a nonboolean, or performing arithmetic on
nonnaturals produces a runtime error.

A variable lookup returning $\mathrm{val}(v)$ returns $v$. A lookup
returning $\mathrm{rec}(y,e,\rho_0)$ resumes $e$ in its captured
environment, extended by the same recursive entry. A missing variable produces
a runtime error. In particular, recursion follows these transitions:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\langle\mathsf{fix}^{a}\ x.\,e,\rho,\kappa\rangle
&\longmapsto
\langle e,\rho[x\mapsto\mathrm{rec}(x,e,\rho)],\kappa\rangle,\\
\langle x,\rho,\kappa\rangle
&\longmapsto
\langle e,\rho_0[y\mapsto\mathrm{rec}(y,e,\rho_0)],\kappa\rangle
\quad\text{if }\rho(x)=\mathrm{rec}(y,e,\rho_0).
\end{aligned}
$$

A final configuration $\langle v,[]\rangle$ is left unchanged by a step.
The evaluator returns immediately upon reaching such a configuration.

## 5. Safety theorems

### Theorem. *Progress*

For every configuration $M$ and type $\tau$,

$$
\vdash_{\mathrm M}M:\tau
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\exists M'.\ M\longmapsto M'.
$$

*Proof.* Inspect the control and continuation typing derivations. The
*Environment lookup* lemma supplies every required environment lookup.
Inverting value typing shows that a function value is a closure, a boolean
value is a boolean, and a natural value is a natural. Thus every required
operation is defined. A final configuration takes its unchanged-state step.

### Theorem. *Preservation*

For every $M$, $M'$, and $\tau$,

$$
\vdash_{\mathrm M}M:\tau\ \land\ M\longmapsto M'
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\vdash_{\mathrm M}M':\tau.
$$

*Proof.* Analyze the typing of the control and the next continuation frame.
Function calls and lets extend a matching environment with a value of the
binder's type. Primitive operations return their declared result types. For
recursion, if $\rho\models\Gamma$ and $\Gamma,x:\sigma\vdash e:\sigma$, then

$$
\rho[x\mapsto\mathrm{rec}(x,e,\rho)]\models\Gamma,x:\sigma.
$$

The resumed body therefore has the required type. In every case the unchanged
remainder of the continuation preserves the final result type $\tau$.

Let $\mathcal E_N(M)$ denote evaluation from configuration $M$ with budget
$N\in\mathbb N$, starting the transition count at zero. A successful result is
$(v,k)$, where $k$ counts executed transitions. A nonfinal configuration with
no remaining budget returns $\mathsf{timeout}$, the fuel-exhaustion diagnostic;
runtime errors are separate outcomes. Final configurations return immediately,
even with zero remaining budget.

### Theorem. *Safety for every evaluation budget*

For every $M$, $\tau$, and $N\in\mathbb N$,

$$
\vdash_{\mathrm M}M:\tau
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\mathcal E_N(M)\in
\{\mathsf{timeout}\}\cup
\{(v,k)\mid k\in\mathbb N\ \land\ \vdash_{\mathrm V}v:\tau\}.
$$

*Proof.* Induct on $N$. A final configuration yields a value of the result type
by inversion of empty-continuation typing. A nonfinal configuration with zero
budget times out. With positive budget, progress supplies a successful step,
preservation types its successor, and the induction hypothesis applies.

### Corollary. *Safety after successful inference*

For every $\Gamma$, $\rho$, $e$, $\tau$, and $N\in\mathbb N$,

$$
\rho\models\Gamma\ \land\ \mathcal I(\Gamma,e)\Downarrow\tau
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\mathcal E_N(\langle e,\rho,[]\rangle)\in
\{\mathsf{timeout}\}\cup
\{(v,k)\mid k\in\mathbb N\ \land\ \vdash_{\mathrm V}v:\tau\}.
$$

*Proof.* By *Soundness of inference*, the expression is typed. Environment
agreement and the empty continuation type its initial configuration. Apply
*Safety for every evaluation budget*. In particular, the only possible
evaluation error after successful inference in a matching environment is
exhaustion of the evaluation budget.

### Theorem. *Soundness of source processing*

Let $u$ be source text. Write $\mathcal P_{\mathrm c}(u)\Downarrow(e,\tau)$
for successful parsing and type checking in the empty context, and
$\mathcal P_{\mathrm r,N}(u)\Downarrow(e,\tau,v,k)$ for successful parsing,
type checking, and evaluation with budget $N$ in the empty environment. Then

$$
\mathcal P_{\mathrm c}(u)\Downarrow(e,\tau)
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\varnothing\vdash e:\tau,
$$

$$
\mathcal P_{\mathrm r,N}(u)\Downarrow(e,\tau,v,k)
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\varnothing\vdash e:\tau\ \land\ \vdash_{\mathrm V}v:\tau.
$$

*Proof.* Apply *Soundness of inference* to the parsed expression. For evaluation,
the empty environment agrees with the empty context, so *Safety after
successful inference* types the returned value. Both statements concern the
expression returned by the source processor.

### Theorem. *A well-typed divergent fixed point*

For any type $\tau$, let $\Omega_\tau=\mathsf{fix}^{\tau}\ x.\,x$. Then

$$
\forall\Gamma.\ \Gamma\vdash\Omega_\tau:\tau,
\qquad
\forall\rho.\ \forall N\in\mathbb N.\
\mathcal E_N(\langle\Omega_\tau,\rho,[]\rangle)=\mathsf{timeout}.
$$

*Proof.* The variable rule gives $\Gamma,x:\tau\vdash x:\tau$, and the
fixed-point rule gives the first conclusion. Evaluation first installs the
suspended binding for $x$. Every subsequent lookup resumes the same expression
in the same extended environment, so the machine repeats a nonfinal
configuration. Induction on the budget gives the second conclusion.
